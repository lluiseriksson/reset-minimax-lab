A. ENTROPY PERTURBATION

A1. Analytic family and its derivative

The tensor channel output is a polynomial in the single real parameter q:

 rho(q) = sum_T (1-q)^|T| q^(n-|T|)
                 [psi_T tensor tau on the complement of T].

It is Hermitian for every real q, including negative q in a neighborhood
of zero. Positivity is needed only for physical q in [0,1]. Consequently,
the open-neighborhood hypothesis of analytic Hermitian perturbation theory
is satisfied even though rho(q) need not be a state at negative q. There
is no requirement to analytically extend entropy itself across q=0.

Differentiating the polynomial gives

 rho(q) = psi + q A + O(q^2),
 A = sum_i E_i(psi) - n psi,
 E_i(psi) = tau at site i tensor psi on the complementary sites.

All remainders here are in a matrix norm in the fixed finite-dimensional
space. Put P=I-psi. Then PAP=sum_i P E_i(psi) P is positive semidefinite.

A2. Degenerate zero eigenvalues

Analytic Hermitian eigenvalue branches exist locally. For an analytic
eigenpair (ell_j(q),u_j(q)) with ell_j(0)=0, differentiating the eigenvector
equation and multiplying by P gives

 PAP u_j(0) = ell'_j(0) u_j(0).

The u_j(0) for these branches form an orthonormal basis of ran(P). Thus the
derivatives are exactly the eigenvalues mu_j of PAP, including multiplicity,
and mu_j>=0. This establishes the candidate's first-order identification;
it does not assume the small eigenvalues remain simple.

If mu_j>0, ell_j(q)=mu_j q+O(q^2), so

 -ell_j(q) ln ell_j(q) = mu_j q ln(1/q) + O(q).

If mu_j=0, analyticity gives ell_j(q)=O(q^2). On the physical side ell_j>=0.
For sufficiently small q, monotonicity of -x ln x on [0,e^-1] then gives
an O(q^2 ln(1/q)) bound. This includes branches of order three or higher,
and branches identically zero. Zero derivative does NOT imply an identically
zero branch; the candidate does not make that mistake. The explicit test
|00><00| with reset |1><1| has a q^2 eigenvalue and checks this issue.

The sole eigenvalue equal to one at q=0 contributes O(q). Therefore

 S(rho(q)) = a q ln(1/q)+O(q),
 a = Tr(PAP) = n - sum_i <v|E_i(psi)|v>.

A3. Independent shorter derivation

The leading entropy coefficient does not actually require analytic
parameterizations of all the small eigenvalues. In the decomposition
span(v) plus its orthogonal complement, rho(q) has block form

 [ 1+q alpha+O(q^2)     O(q) ]
 [       O(q)           O(q) ],     alpha=<v|A|v>.

The isolated eigenvalue ell_*(q) near one is
ell_*(q)=1+q alpha+O(q^2). For example, its lower block eigenvector is O(q)
times its upper component because the lower block resolvent near one is
bounded; substitution in the upper equation yields this formula.

Let delta_mass=1-ell_*(q)=a q+O(q^2), where a=-alpha. With N=d^n>1,
normalize the other eigenvalues to a probability vector r when delta_mass>0.
The exact identity

 S(rho(q)) = h(delta_mass)+delta_mass H(r),
 0 <= H(r) <= ln(N-1),

proves the same expansion. If a>0, h(delta_mass)=a q ln(1/q)+O(q).
If a=0, delta_mass=O(q^2) and the entropy is O(q^2 ln(1/q)). For N=1
the entropy is identically zero. This alternate derivation rules out an
unnoticed degeneracy problem in the claimed leading coefficient.

A4. Overlap and subtraction

Across site i, write v=sum_k sqrt(p_k) |a_k>|b_k>. The complementary
marginal is sum_k p_k |b_k><b_k|. Direct evaluation gives

 <v|tau tensor psi_complement|v>
   = sum_k p_k^2 <a_k|tau|a_k>
   = Tr(tau psi_i^2).

There is no transpose or commutation assumption on tau. For the fixed
number t_i=Tr(tau psi_i) in [0,1],

 h(q t_i)=t_i q ln(1/q)+O(q).

This is immediate for t_i=0; for t_i>0 the other linear term contains the
fixed finite number -t_i ln t_i. Combining all terms gives

 a-n+sum_i t_i = sum_i Tr[tau(psi_i-psi_i^2)] = Delta,

which is exactly Claim A. The linear term -n q S(tau) has correctly been
absorbed into O(q). Finite dimension ensures S(tau) is finite.

B. THE Delta=0 SUPPORT REDUCTION

B1. Noncommuting positive factors

For K_i=psi_i(I-psi_i)>=0,

 Tr(tau K_i) = ||K_i^(1/2) tau^(1/2)||_HS^2 >=0.

If their sum vanishes, each term vanishes and K_i^(1/2) tau^(1/2)=0.
Hence supp(tau) is contained in ker(K_i). This implication is valid even
when tau and psi_i do not commute. It is stronger than a statement about
diagonal entries and is the correct support conclusion.

B2. Pure sites must be separated first

If psi_i is pure, Schmidt rank at that cut is one, so the global pure state
factors at that site. Repeating over F, all sites with pure marginals,

 psi = (tensor_{i in F} phi_i) tensor chi_E,

where chi_E is a pure state on the other sites. Its one-site marginals
are the original psi_i for i in E and are all mixed. A mixed density matrix
has every positive eigenvalue strictly less than one. Thus, for i in E,

 ker[psi_i(I-psi_i)] = ker(psi_i),
 supp(tau) subset ker(psi_i), and t_i=0.

The separation of F is essential: for a pure marginal psi_i(I-psi_i)=0,
so Delta=0 imposes no orthogonality restriction there. The candidate
handles this distinction correctly. E can be empty; a singleton E cannot
arise from this pure factorization, but the proof needs no special case.

B3. Orthogonal patterns, including entangled retained subsets

Let Q_i project onto supp(psi_i) for i in E, and let Q_tau project onto
supp(tau). We have Q_i Q_tau=0. For any retained subset T of E, its
reduced state chi_T is supported on tensor_{i in T} ran(Q_i). One way to
see this is that each complementary local projection has zero expectation
in the corresponding positive reduced state; therefore it annihilates its
support. This argument does not require chi_T to be a product state.

The branch for T is supported on

 (tensor_{i in T} ran(Q_i)) tensor
 (tensor_{i in E\T} ran(Q_tau)).

Two distinct subsets differ at some site, where these local subspaces are
orthogonal. Their global branch supports are therefore orthogonal. The
eigenvalues of their weighted mixture are the union of their weighted
individual eigenvalues. This gives the exact entropy identity

 S(R_q^tensor |E|(chi_E))
   = |E| h(q)+|E| q S(tau)+E_{T subset E} S(chi_T),

where each site belongs to T independently with probability 1-q. Zero-weight
branches at q=0 or q=1 cause no problem, with 0 ln 0=0 and S(chi_empty)=0.
This expectation is nonnegative. The candidate's entropy computation is
therefore exact, not a lower bound obtained by subadditivity.

B4. Explicit pure-site binary-error argument

For a pure factor phi, let J be the retention flag. The cq entropy identity is

 S((1-q)phi+q tau)=h(q)+q S(tau)-H(J|B).

Measure {phi,I-phi}, with outcome Y; guess retention on phi and replacement
on I-phi. The error event occurs only when replacement was chosen and the
phi outcome appears. Hence e=q Tr(phi tau). Data processing gives
H(J|B)<=H(J|Y). Because J is binary and the guess is determined by Y, J is
determined by Y and the binary error bit Z. Therefore

 H(J|Y)=H(Z|Y)<=H(Z)=h(e).

This derivation is valid even if e>1/2, and requires neither a uniform flag
nor an optimal decision rule. Thus every pure factor has G_phi(q)>=0.

In fact the proof gives the useful exact decomposition

 G(q)=sum_{i in F} G_phi_i(q)+E_{T subset E} S(chi_T) >=0.

This proves Claim B for all q in [0,1], including F empty and E empty.
For a simple check, take a two-site Schmidt state with weights p,1-p on
levels 0,1 and a reset supported on orthogonal levels 2,3. Then exactly
G(q)=2q(1-q)h(p), for any reset spectrum on levels 2,3.

C. COROLLARY AND EXACT SCOPE

If Delta>0, the fixed-tuple remainder satisfies |r(q)|<=Cq for small q.
Thus G(q)>=q[Delta ln(1/q)-C]>=0 for sufficiently small q. At q=0,
G(0)=S(psi)=0. If Delta=0, Claim B applies on the entire interval.
The claimed existence of q_0>0 follows.

The constants C, the neighborhood where the expansion holds, and hence
q_0 can depend on n,d,psi,tau. Taking an infimum of these neighborhoods
over changing inputs, dimensions, or blocklengths is not justified. Nothing
here proves the all-q conjecture for Delta>0, excludes q-dependent-input
counterexamples, or permits a regularized capacity conclusion.
