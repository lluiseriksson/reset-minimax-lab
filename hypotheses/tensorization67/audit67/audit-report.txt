1. PRELIMINARY BOUNDS AND DOMAIN

Set b_i=Tr(tau psi_i^2), a=sum_i(1-b_i), and T=sum_i t_i.
Since each marginal satisfies 0<=psi_i^2<=psi_i<=I,

 0<=b_i<=t_i<=1,
 0<=a<=n,
 Delta=T-sum_i b_i=a-n+T>=0,
 Delta<=a.

The traces of products are ordered because tau is positive; no commutation
of tau and psi_i is assumed. The useful combined inequality is

 0<=Delta<=a<=n.

Also 0<=S(tau)<infinity in fixed finite dimension. These facts justify all
uses of positivity and the constants below.

2. PROOF OF THE PURITY-BASED BOUND (P)

Assume first 0<q<=1/(2n). Let r=(1-q)^n and p=1-r. Both p and 1-p are
strictly positive. The retention-pattern expansion separates the no-reset
branch, giving

 rho=(1-p)psi+p sigma,

where sigma is a density matrix: it is the normalized mixture of the
remaining branches with nonnegative weights. Let psi=|v><v| and
f=<v|sigma|v> in [0,1]. Since Tr(sigma^2)<=1,

 Tr(rho^2)
   =(1-p)^2+2p(1-p)f+p^2 Tr(sigma^2)
   <=(1-p)^2+2p(1-p)f+p^2
   =1-2p(1-p)(1-f).

Let lambda=lambda_max(rho) in [0,1] and e=1-lambda. Then

 e-(1-lambda^2)/2=(1-lambda)^2/2>=0,
 lambda^2<=Tr(rho^2).

Consequently, with exactly the signs appearing in the candidate,

 e>=(1-lambda^2)/2>=(1-Tr(rho^2))/2
   >=p(1-p)(1-f).

If F=<v|rho|v>, then F=1-p+pf and 1-F=p(1-f), so

 e>=(1-p)(1-F).

For each replacement subset U, let rho_U be the corresponding normalized
branch and w_U=q^|U|(1-q)^(n-|U|). Its overlap F_U=<v|rho_U|v> lies in [0,1].
Because the weights sum to one,

 1-F=sum_U w_U(1-F_U).

Every summand is nonnegative. The U=empty contribution vanishes, while
the n single-replacement terms give

 1-F>=q(1-q)^(n-1) sum_i[1-<v|E_i(psi)|v>]
     =q(1-q)^(n-1) a.

The equality uses <v|E_i(psi)|v>=Tr(tau psi_i^2). Directly, in a Schmidt
decomposition v=sum_k sqrt(s_k)|u_k>|w_k> across site i, the overlap is
sum_k s_k^2 <u_k|tau|u_k>, exactly that trace. Hence

 e>=a q(1-q)^(2n-1)=:x>=0.

In the other direction, the Rayleigh quotient and the positive no-reset
branch imply lambda>=F>=r. Bernoulli's inequality gives

 e<=1-r=p<=nq<=1/2.

Therefore 0<=x<=e<=1/2, including the range needed for binary entropy
monotonicity. If e>0, normalize the nonlargest eigenvalues to a probability
vector u. The exact entropy identity is

 S(rho)=h(e)+e H(u)>=h(e).

For e=0, rho is pure and the same inequality holds with h(0)=0. Binary
entropy is increasing on [0,1/2], so

 S(rho)>=h(e)>=h(x)=h(a q(1-q)^(2n-1)).

This proves (P). At q=0, the mixture sigma is not defined, but it is not
needed: rho=psi, x=0 and both sides of (P) are zero. The upper endpoint
q=1/(2n) is included throughout. No division by e occurs in the e=0 case.

3. THE EXPLICIT SUFFICIENT NEIGHBORHOOD

Assume Delta>0 and set

 q_0=min{1/(2n), Delta/(4n^2),
         exp[-2n(ln n+S(tau)+1)/Delta]}.

All three quantities are strictly positive real numbers; n>=1 gives
ln n>=0 and S(tau)>=0. Thus q_0>0 mathematically. Extremely small values
could underflow in a numerical implementation; this does not affect the
exact existence statement. No computational underflow guarantee is claimed.

Fix 0<q<=q_0 and abbreviate L=ln(1/q)>0. Since a>=Delta>0 and q<1,
the earlier bound x>0 also gives e>0, so ln(1/e) is well-defined.
Bernoulli's inequality with exponent 2n-1 yields

 e>=a q(1-q)^(2n-1)
   >=a q-a(2n-1)q^2
   >=a q-2n^2 q^2=:b,

where a<=n and 2n-1<=2n were used in the last step. Furthermore,
2n^2 q<=Delta/2<=a/2, so b>=a q/2>0. This explicitly justifies the
nonnegative lower coefficient used in the candidate.

The grouping identity above and h(e)>=e ln(1/e) give S(rho)>=e ln(1/e).
Since e<=nq<=1/2,

 ln(1/e)>=ln(1/(nq))>0.

Multiplying first by e>=0 and then using e>=b gives

 S(rho)>=e ln(1/e)
        >=e ln(1/(nq))
        >=b ln(1/(nq)).

There is no reliance here on monotonicity of the function e ln(1/e),
which would be unsafe across its maximum at e=1/euler. The candidate's
inequality is valid because it separately bounds the logarithm and its
nonnegative coefficient. Expanding with ln(1/(nq))=L-ln n gives

 S(rho)>=q[aL-a ln n-2n^2 qL+2n^2 q ln n]
        >=q[aL-a ln n-2n^2 qL],

where the last discarded term is nonnegative because ln n>=0.

For the desired conjecture right-hand side, put

 B(q)=n[q S(tau)+h(q)]-sum_i h(qt_i).

For 0<q<1, the elementary bounds

 h(q)<=qL+q,
 h(qt_i)>=qt_i ln(1/(qt_i))

hold, with the second right-hand side interpreted as zero when t_i=0.
The first uses -(1-q)ln(1-q)<=q; the second drops the nonnegative other
term of the binary entropy. Thus, with 0 ln 0=0,

 B(q)<=(n-T)qL+nq[S(tau)+1]+q sum_i t_i ln(t_i)
     <=(n-T)qL+nq[S(tau)+1].

The sign of this discarded term is correct: every t_i ln(t_i)<=0.
Subtracting this upper bound on B from the lower bound on S yields

 G(q)>=q[Delta L-a ln n-2n^2 qL-n(S(tau)+1)].

Because 2n^2 q<=Delta/2, L>0, a<=n and ln n>=0,

 G(q)>=q[(Delta/2)L-n ln n-n(S(tau)+1)].

The exponential restriction in q_0 is equivalent to

 L>=2n(ln n+S(tau)+1)/Delta.

It therefore makes the final bracket nonnegative, proving G(q)>=0.
Every inequality is non-strict where needed, so q=q_0 is included. At q=0,
G(0)=S(psi)=0, establishing the full closed interval [0,q_0].

4. EDGE CASES AND NONCLAIMS

If a=0, (P) reduces to S(rho)>=0, with no logarithm of zero required.
In fact Delta<=a implies Delta=0, so this case is excluded from the q_0
formula. The same applies to d=1. For n=1 the only marginal is the pure
input and Delta=0; (P) still applies, while the Delta>0 neighborhood has no
instance. Inputs with t_i=0 or t_i=1 and rank-deficient resets are covered
by the stated conventions and the proofs above.

The formula must not be evaluated at Delta=0. The candidate appropriately
refers that case to the separate support argument in lemma66. No version
of that argument is needed for this audit's Delta>0 conclusion.

The quantitative neighborhood depends on n, Delta and S(tau). It need not
remain bounded away from zero when these parameters vary, particularly
as Delta approaches zero. This proof supplies neither the all-q conjecture
for general entangled inputs nor any regularized or energy-uniform capacity
result. It does not assess whether the bounds are known or novel.
